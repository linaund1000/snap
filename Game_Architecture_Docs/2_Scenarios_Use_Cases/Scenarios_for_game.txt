SCENARIOS FOR GAME - DEEP ANALYSIS
==================================
1. Game scenarios represent the high-level narrative and systemic arcs the player can experience.
2. Currently, the scenarios in GPOyun are intended to be emergent, but the architecture restricts them to hardcoded paths.
3. Scenario 1: The Gossip Catalyst.
4. Intent: The player photographs a secret meeting, publishes it, and the town turns on the individuals.
5. Reality: The system cannot recognize "secret meetings." It only recognizes `PhotoSubject` IDs.
6. The `NewspaperManager` randomly assigns the "Scandal" category to the photo.
7. Thus, the town turns on an individual completely at random, rather than based on the player's intentional timing.
8. To fix Scenario 1, the `PhotoData` must capture the *Action* the NPC was performing (e.g., `SocializeAction` with a rival).
9. Scenario 2: The Hero's Rise.
10. Intent: The player captures an NPC doing a good deed; their relationship scores max out.
11. Reality: The Utility AI currently contains no "Good Deed" actions.
12. NPCs only possess selfish actions (`Eat`, `Sleep`, `ReadNews`, `Socialize`).
13. To fix Scenario 2, altruistic actions (e.g., `GiveGiftAction`, `HelpFallenAction`) must be implemented in the Utility AI.
14. Scenario 3: The Group Hangout Dynamics.
15. Intent: A cluster of friends gathers at the fountain, creating a highly dense social event to photograph.
16. Reality: `GroupHangoutAction` forces followers to lerp to a coordinate.
17. They do not face each other, nor do their animations synchronize.
18. To fix Scenario 3, `SocialGroupManager` needs a formation handler (e.g., Circular Formation) and IK LookAt targets.
19. Scenario 4: The Photo Bomb.
20. Intent: The player tries to photograph a subject, but a rival jumps into the frame to ruin it.
21. Reality: The `PhotoScorer` algorithm evaluates the exact center of the screen via a single raycast.
22. It cannot detect a secondary NPC ruining the shot.
23. To fix Scenario 4, the camera system must evaluate all `PhotoSubject` bounds within the frustum and penalize scores for overlapping unwanted subjects.
24. Scenario 5: The Ripple Effect (Chain Reactions).
25. Intent: NPC A hates NPC B. NPC A reads a good article about NPC B and becomes angry at the newspaper.
26. Reality: The `AppraisalEngine` only modifies the direct relationship with the photo subject.
27. It lacks second-order relational shifts.
28. To fix Scenario 5, the math must loop through the reader's friends: "If my friend hates you, and the news praises you, I hate the news."
29. Scenario 6: NPC Fleeing from Flash.
30. Intent: Neurotic NPCs run away when a camera flashes in their face.
31. Reality: The `NPCSensoryMatrix` detects the flash, but the FSM cannot interrupt the active Utility AI action.
32. The NPC will stand still, process the "Flee" emotion internally, and continue eating.
33. To fix Scenario 6, the `ReactAction` must have an absolute override priority, immediately canceling the current pathfinding target.
34. Scenario 7: The Silent Town (Failure State).
35. Intent: If the player stops taking photos, the town slowly stagnates and becomes bored.
36. Reality: If the player takes no photos, the `NewspaperManager` either crashes or publishes null data during the Morning phase.
37. The Needs decay system continues, but social shifts completely halt.
38. To fix Scenario 7, the system must generate fallback global news (e.g., "Heatwave incoming") to keep the simulation alive without player input.
39. Scenario 8: The Late Night Encounter.
40. Intent: Players can sneak around at night to catch rare interactions.
41. Reality: At Night, `TimeManager` triggers `WalkingHome`. All NPCs go indoors and idle infinitely.
42. The night phase is entirely devoid of gameplay or systemic variation.
43. To fix Scenario 8, nocturnal personalities must be introduced via `NPCPersonalityData`, allowing some NPCs to wander specifically at night.
44. The scenarios demonstrate that GPOyun is a simulation without agency.
45. Emergent gameplay requires rules, not randomness.
46. The player cannot craft a scenario if the system relies on `Random.Range` for outcomes.
47. Every scenario must be tethered to deterministic constraints.
48. If I take a picture of Leo talking to Zoe, it must ALWAYS trigger a gossip event.
49. The architecture must bridge the gap between the player's mechanical input (taking a photo) and the narrative output (the town's reaction).
50. Currently, that bridge is broken by synchronous I/O, God-object FSMs, and random number generators.
